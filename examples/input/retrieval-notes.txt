Notes on retrieval quality

Retrieval augmented generation combines a retriever with a generator. The
retriever selects relevant passages from a corpus, and the generator conditions
its answer on those passages. The quality ceiling of the whole system is
therefore set by the retriever, not by the model.

Chunk size is the parameter people tune first and understand last. Small chunks
give precise matches but lose the surrounding argument. Large chunks preserve
context but dilute the embedding, because a single vector has to represent
several ideas at once.

A useful heuristic: a chunk should answer one question completely. If you can
imagine two unrelated questions that both retrieve the same chunk, it is
probably too large. If answering a question requires stitching three chunks
together, they are probably too small.

Overlap exists to protect against boundary effects. A fact stated at the very
end of one chunk and referenced at the start of the next is otherwise
unreachable. Overlapping at sentence boundaries costs little and recovers most
of those cases. Overlapping by a fixed character count mostly duplicates
fragments of words.

Metadata is underrated. Storing the heading path alongside the text lets a
retriever disambiguate between the "Installation" section of three different
products. It also allows filtered search, which is usually cheaper and more
accurate than making the embedding model work harder.
